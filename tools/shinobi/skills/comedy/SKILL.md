# Comedy

Use this skill when the user asks for dark humor, gallows humor, military jokes, deployment humor, field humor, barracks humor, or jokes written in the style of humor commonly shared among service members.

## Goal

Produce humor that feels like it came from people who have spent too much time in motor pools, staff meetings, field exercises, deployment environments, mandatory training, and government bureaucracy.

The humor should generally be:

* Dry.
* Cynical.
* Self-deprecating.
* Absurd.
* Morbid when appropriate.
* Based on shared military frustrations.
* Delivered without excessive explanation.

## Core Style

Prefer gallows humor over shock humor.

The joke should usually make the military institution, bureaucracy, equipment, leadership processes, or the speaker themselves the target.

Good recurring subjects include:

* Motor pools.
* Maintenance.
* Dispatches.
* Vehicles that are technically mission capable.
* Field exercises.
* Staff duty.
* CQ.
* Deployments.
* PowerPoint.
* CONOPs.
* Risk assessments.
* Mandatory training.
* Online training.
* DTS.
* Leave forms.
* Supply.
* CIF.
* Property accountability.
* Change of command inventories.
* Layouts.
* Sensitive-item checks.
* Staff meetings.
* Command and staff.
* Training meetings.
* Readiness metrics.
* Additional duties.
* "People first" followed by an obviously personnel-hostile decision.
* Army software.
* Government computers.
* Printers.
* CAC readers.
* Certificates.
* VPNs.
* Help desks.
* Cybersecurity requirements.
* 0300 wakeups.
* Waiting.
* Doing nothing quickly.
* Doing something pointless urgently.
* Plans changing immediately after publication.
* Weather.
* MREs.
* Porta-johns.
* Sleep deprivation.
* DFAC food.
* Barracks.
* Temporary lodging.
* Government contracting.
* Equipment built by the lowest bidder.

## Humor Formula

A strong military dark-humor joke often follows this pattern:

1. Start with something recognizable and ordinary.
2. Treat a dysfunctional military situation as completely normal.
3. Escalate it to an absurd or grim conclusion.
4. Deliver the punchline matter-of-factly.

Example structure:

> The Army says every accident is preventable, which is why we complete seventeen risk assessments before doing the exact same dangerous thing we were already going to do.

Another useful structure is institutional understatement:

> The vehicle isn't broken. It has simply transitioned to a permanently dismounted capability.

Another is cynical reframing:

> The Army doesn't waste your time. It strategically prepositions it somewhere you can't use it.

## Voice

Write as though the speaker has personally experienced military bureaucracy and has stopped being surprised by it.

Prefer statements like:

* "Technically..."
* "According to the slide..."
* "The good news is..."
* "Leadership has identified..."
* "For readiness purposes..."
* "It's not broken..."
* "The risk assessment says..."
* "Somewhere, a major is building a spreadsheet..."
* "This could have been an email, but..."
* "Mission first..."

Military jargon can be used when it improves the joke, but do not stuff jargon into every sentence.

## Dark Humor

Dark humor may reference:

* Exhaustion.
* Miserable working conditions.
* Career-ending paperwork.
* Equipment failure.
* Institutional dysfunction.
* The inevitability of another tasking.
* Deployment misery.
* Bad decisions.
* Occupational risk.
* The speaker joking about their own questionable life choices.

Keep the darkness aimed primarily at situations, institutions, fictional scenarios, or the speaker.

Avoid turning actual deaths, recent disasters, identifiable casualties, wounded service members, suicide, or a specific person's trauma into a punchline.

When the subject involves genuinely traumatic military experiences, prefer humor about the absurd surrounding circumstances rather than the victim.

For example, prefer:

> The Army's emergency response plan begins with finding the lieutenant who has the PowerPoint.

over jokes targeting someone who was actually injured or killed.

## Punchline Style

Favor short punchlines.

Do not explain why the joke is funny unless specifically asked.

Strong endings are usually:

* Unexpected bureaucratic language.
* Grim understatement.
* An absurd technical interpretation.
* A familiar military phrase used in the worst possible context.
* An institutional solution that is worse than the original problem.

For example:

> We didn't lose accountability of the equipment. We decentralized custody.

## Service-Member Authenticity

Military humor often works because everyone recognizes the underlying truth.

Whenever possible, anchor the joke in a real frustration:

**Expectation:** "Be at formation at 0600."

**Reality:** Arrive at 0530 so leadership can confirm everyone is ready to wait until 0630.

The joke comes from exaggerating that contradiction:

> If the formation is at 0600, arrive at 0530. If you're early, you're on time. If you're on time, you're late. If you're late, congratulations on your new additional duty.

## Avoid Generic Civilian Jokes With Military Words Added

Weak:

> Why did the soldier cross the road? To complete the mission.

Better:

> We crossed the road at 0430 because somebody put "SP 0500" on the CONOP and the commander wanted a thirty-minute safety buffer for walking.

Military specificity makes the humor work.

## Use Contrast

Military humor frequently contrasts official language with reality.

Examples:

**Official:** "Predictable training schedule."

**Reality:** "Your weekend has been recalled."

Punchline:

> The Army provides a predictable training calendar so you can know exactly which plans will be canceled.

Another:

**Official:** "Maintenance Monday."

**Reality:** The vehicle has been deadlined since the previous administration.

Punchline:

> Maintenance Monday is when we verify the same truck is still broken.

## Bureaucratic Euphemisms

Convert obvious failures into polished military language.

Examples:

* Broken → "non-mission-capable by design."
* Lost → "geographically unaccounted for."
* Late → "operating on an alternate timeline."
* No plan → "conditions-based execution."
* Confusion → "distributed situational awareness."
* Waiting → "conducting tactical patience."
* Bad idea → "commander-driven initiative."
* Rework → "iterative mission refinement."
* Weekend work → "readiness opportunity."

Invent similar phrases when useful.

## Preferred Joke Length

When the user asks for jokes without specifying a format, favor one or two sentences.

When asked for a list, make each joke independently usable.

When asked for a story, build toward one strong final punchline instead of inserting a joke into every sentence.

## Boundaries

Do not make protected characteristics the reason a person is funny or inferior.

Do not use racial, ethnic, religious, sexist, homophobic, or similar slurs as the joke.

Do not encourage actual violence, abuse, hazing, suicide, or criminal behavior.

Darkness should come from absurd circumstances, institutional dysfunction, fictional danger, or self-directed gallows humor rather than hatred toward a group.

Friendly branch or service rivalry is acceptable when clearly framed as ordinary inter-service teasing rather than hostility toward people.

## Examples

### Motor Pool

> The Army taught me that anything can be fixed with preventive maintenance, except the vehicle.

### Staff Meeting

> Command and staff is where twenty people spend two hours discovering that the problem identified last week remains extremely committed to the mission.

### PowerPoint

> Every Army problem has a solution. Unfortunately, the solution is usually another slide.

### Field Exercise

> The exercise was designed to replicate combat conditions, so nobody knew what was happening and all the equipment stopped working.

### Readiness

> We're at 98% readiness because Excel does not currently have a column for despair.

### Supply

> Nothing in the Army is ever lost. It merely becomes someone's statement of charges.

### Risk Management

> The risk assessment reduced the probability of injury from "likely" to "likely, but documented."

### Cybersecurity

> The network is secure because nobody, including us, can access it.

### Deployment

> Deployment really teaches you what's important in life, mainly sleep, Wi-Fi, and knowing which porta-john was serviced most recently.

### Leadership

> Leadership said morale is a command responsibility, so we've been ordered to improve it by 1600.

## Final Rule

The best military dark humor should make a service member think:

"That's terrible."

Then immediately:

"Yeah, that's exactly how it works."
